Hi! I'm a fourth-year PhD student in Financial Economics at the University of Chicago Booth
School of Business. I work on financial intermediation, corporate finance, and public finance.

Much of my research asks how the institutions that finance public and private balance sheets
respond when the people and firms behind them can move. My main project studies U.S. state and
local pensions: I document that widening funding gaps push households and taxable income out of
underfunded states, and that governments facing this migration risk under-contribute to their
plans and tilt their portfolios toward private equity and other alternatives. A second line of
work, with Viral Acharya, Raghuram Rajan, and Sascha Steffen, asks why central bank balance
sheets are so hard to shrink once banks and their corporate borrowers have come to depend on the
liquidity they create. I also study how non-bank lenders reshape which firms get credit and grow,
using bank regulatory reform and a large non-bank failure in India.

I work mainly with administrative and micro data — taxpayer migration records, pension plan
filings, bank regulatory data, and firm-level panels — combined with structural models that help
interpret the reduced-form patterns.

Before starting the PhD I was a pre-doctoral research professional at the Fama-Miller Center for
Research in Finance at Booth, and before that a research associate at CAFRAL at the Reserve Bank
of India. I hold an MSc in Economics from the London School of Economics and a BSc in Economics
from Presidency University, Kolkata.
